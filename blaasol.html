Blaa Sol Festival App

Background

BLÅ SOL is a music festival held annually in Randers, Denmark, celebrating local artists and bringing together thousands of visitors every year. As part of our second semester final project, we were challenged to design a digital solution that would improve the festival experience while creating value for both visitors and the organisers.

Problem

Music festivals are highly social experiences, but staying connected with friends can quickly become frustrating. Large crowds, busy schedules, and unreliable messaging make it difficult for groups to meet up or keep track of each other throughout the day.

Our research revealed that visitors needed a solution that supported social coordination without becoming another complicated app to manage. The challenge was to design an experience that felt helpful, intuitive, and seamlessly integrated into the festival experience.

Goal

Design new functionalities in the existing BLÅ SOL app that supports social coordination in a simple, intuitive, and non-intrusive way, helping visitors stay connected, meet up easily and enjoy the festival together.

01.1 Planning

The first thing we did was focusing on organizing our workflow as a team. We created a project timeline with milestones and internal deadlines that preceded the official submission date, ensuring we had enough time for iterations and refinement.

To facilitate collaboration, we also mapped each team member's availability in a shared calendar. This made it easier to schedule meetings and divide tasks efficiently despite different schedules. This gave us dedicated time for feedback and iterations instead of rushing to finish before the final deadline.

01.2 Foundational Research

Before defining the problem, we wanted to understand how people experience festivals and where existing digital solutions fall short. Since BLÅ SOL had not yet taken place, we combined desk research, netnography, observations, and interviews to build a comprehensive understanding of our users and their environment.

Desk Research and Netnography

We began by researching music festivals, festival applications, and current digital solutions available on the market. This helped us understand common features, identify recurring pain points, and explore opportunities where a new solution could provide additional value.

We also gathered information about BLÅ SOL itself, including its target audience, organisational identity, communication channels, and visual language. This helped us identify how the solution could support the festival while remaining consistent with its existing identity.

To complement our desk research, we analysed discussions and content shared on social media platforms where festival visitors communicate and exchange experiences. By observing conversations, comments, and user-generated content, we gained insight into visitors' expectations, frustrations, and behaviours before, during and after festivals. This helped us identify recurring themes that could later be explored in interviews.

Observations

Because the 2026 edition of BLÅ SOL had not yet taken place, we conducted contextual observations using photos and videos from Grøn Festival, a Danish music festival with a similar atmosphere and audience.

We focused on how people moved through the festival, interacted with friends, and used their phones in real situations.

Interviews

To ensure our findings reflected BLÅ SOL's intended audience, we interviewed Danish festival-goers aged 18–34 who regularly attend festivals with friends.

Although they were not necessarily BLÅ SOL attendees, they represented the same target group and shared comparable behaviours, motivations, and challenges.

Across the interviews, participants consistently described festivals as highly social experiences where spending time together was one of their main motivations for attending. However, they also highlighted recurring frustrations such as getting separated from friends in crowded areas, struggling to coordinate meeting points, relying on messaging apps that often became inconvenient, and having plans change spontaneously throughout the day.

These insights confirmed that the challenge was not simply about navigation, but about supporting social coordination in a way that felt intuitive and non-intrusive.

02.1 User Persona

After synthesizing our research findings, we created a persona to represent our primary user group. Rather than describing a single individual, Ella combines the recurring behaviors, goals, frustrations, and motivations we identified across interviews and observations.

The persona helped us keep real user needs at the centre of every design decision throughout the project.

Emma, a 24-year-old international student visiting Aarhus, reflects the needs of users looking for authentic local experiences. Her preference for mobile navigation, visual inspiration, and quick access to information directly influenced the website's responsive design, interactive map, and emphasis on photography and storytelling.

02.2 Values

Based on our research findings, we defined four core values to guide the design of our solution. These values ensured that every feature and interaction aligned with both user needs and BLÅ SOL's vision of creating a memorable, community-focused festival experience.

These values became a reference point throughout the design process, helping us evaluate ideas and ensure that every feature supported both user needs and the overall festival experience.

02.3 Define the User’s Needs and Problems

After synthesising our research, we transformed the users' main pain points into How Might We questions. This method encouraged us to reframe problems as opportunities, allowing us to explore multiple design directions before committing to a solution.

Rather than searching for a single answer, the HMW exercise helped us generate ideas that aligned with both user needs and the goals of BLÅ SOL.

HOW MIGHT WE...

Reduce the need for constant messaging?
  • Live friend location sharing
  • Smart meeting points
  • “Find my group” feature
  • Time-based meetup reminders
  • Arrival notifications
Minimize battery consumption?
  • Periodic GPS updates instead of continuous tracking
  • Battery-efficient location sharing
  • Accessible color contrast for low-power display modes
Make meeting points more intuitive?
  • Clearly labelled map pins
  • Landmark-based meeting points
  • Visual cues instead of vague location descriptions
Create a frictionless experience?
  • Simple and intuitive interface
  • Minimal interactions
  • Focus on essential features
Encourage spontaneous social experiences?
  • Shared group schedules
  • Collaborative planning
  • Features that support discovery while staying connected
Keep users connected without requiring constant attention?
  • Smart notifications
  • Quick preset messages
  • Passive updates instead of manual check-ins
Make the app reliable in poor network conditions?
  • Downloadable festival map
  • Lightweight map assets
  • Reduced update frequency
  • Last known location indicator

To ensure our solution addressed real user needs, we used a Value Proposition Canvas to connect our research findings with potential features. By mapping users' goals, frustrations, and desired outcomes, we were able to evaluate whether each design decision provided meaningful value.

ThE USEr:

Jobs:

Stay connected with friends throughout the festival, find friends quickly after getting separated, navigate the festival with ease, decide what to do and where to go together, enjoy a stress-free shared festival experience.

Gains:

Easily reconnect with friends, spend more time enjoying the festival together, reduce stress and unnecessary coordination, access information quickly and intuitively, feel free to be spontaneous without losing the group.

Pains:

Losing friends in crowded areas, difficulty finding each other again, relying on constant messaging and phone calls, confusing or unclear meeting locations, existing festival apps feel unnecessary or too complex, concerns about battery consumption.

ThE SOLUTION:

Products & Services:

Interactive social map with friend locations, smart meeting points, group-based coordination features, "I'm Here" quick location sharing, optional shared moments and group interactions.

Pain Relievers:

Reduce the need for constant messaging, make reconnecting with friends faster and easier, simplify navigation in crowded environments, provide lightweight and battery-conscious interactions, deliver clear real-time coordination.

Gain Creators:

Encourage spontaneous yet connected experiences, strengthen social interaction throughout the festival, increase enjoyment by reducing coordination effort, save time and minimize stress, create a intuitive experience that adds value without extra complexity.

02.4 Design Process and User Testing

• Sketches and User Flow

With our research findings clearly defined, we moved into the ideation phase. We began by sketching a wide range of concepts individually before discussing them as a team. This allowed us to quickly explore different layouts, interactions, and feature ideas without being constrained by visual details.

• Visual Identity

We created a moodboard to capture the feeling we wanted users to experience throughout the app. Inspired by music festivals, it focused on vibrant lighting, energetic crowds, shared moments, and a sense of community. This helped us define the emotional direction of the experience before designing individual screens.

Unlike the moodboard, the style tile was based on BLÅ SOL's existing visual identity. We analysed the festival's branding, colours, typography, and graphical elements to ensure that the new features felt like a natural extension of the current application rather than a separate product.

• Wireframes

With the user flow defined, we created low-fidelity wireframes to test the layout, navigation, and core interactions before focusing on visual design.

To make the experience intuitive, we drew inspiration from familiar apps our target audience already uses:

  • Find My friend location sharing and map interactions.
  • Spotify collaborative group scheduling inspired by shared playlists.
  • Snapchat lightweight social map and location-sharing experience.

Using established interaction patterns helped reduce the learning curve and create a familiar, user-friendly experience.

02.8 Usability Testing

To validate the information architecture and user flow, we conducted think-aloud usability testing with six participants using our low-fidelity prototype. Participants completed realistic tasks while verbalizing their thoughts, allowing us to identify usability issues before moving to the final design.

Key Findings

  • Users found the overall navigation intuitive and familiar.
  • The map page and group coordination features were well received.
  • Some interactive elements, such as the friends list, did not appear clickable.
  • The account and group pages were difficult to locate.
  • The location-sharing flow was not immediately clear.

Design Iterations

  • Increased the visibility of the Profile and Group pages.
  • Moved the Group page to the bottom navigation for quicker access.
  • Improved the clickability of friends on the map and in lists.
  • Simplified the location-sharing flow and meeting point interactions.
  • Replaced the map with BLÅ SOL's updated festival map provided by the client for better clarity.
  • Expanded the group system to allow users to join multiple groups instead of just one, making the app more flexible for different social circles and festival activities.

Take-aways and Further Development Ideas

Working on BLÅ SOL strengthened my understanding of how research drives design decisions. Throughout the project, I learned the importance of validating ideas early, iterating based on user feedback, and designing within the constraints of an existing product rather than starting from scratch.

As a team, we discovered that even small usability issues can significantly affect the overall experience. The testing sessions led to meaningful improvements, including simplifying navigation, refining location sharing, and expanding the group functionality to support multiple groups instead of just one.

This project reinforced the value of designing around real user needs instead of assumptions and gave me valuable experience collaborating on a complete UX process from research to a functional prototype.
