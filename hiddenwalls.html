Background

Hidden Walls is a responsive web experience that shows three hidden street art spots in Aarhus. Through storytelling, photography and interactive design, users are encouraged to discover places that are often overlooked while exploring the city.

Problem

Street art breaks free from the traditional idea that art has to be displayed in a museum to be considered valid. However, since it’s often associated with vandalism, it can be stigmatized and overlooked. As a result, many unique and beautiful spots remain hidden from both tourists and locals. Existing map services may provide directions, but they fail to communicate the culture, atmosphere and useful information about these artworks, making it hard to fully understand them.

Goal

The goal was to design a responsive website that inspires and assists exploration through intuitive user experience and strong visual storytelling. The project focused on translating user research into a solution that helps visitors connect with one of the most hidden parts of the city.

Overview

01.1 Foundational research 01.2 Target group 01.3 Problem statement

01.1 Foundational Research

To create a solution that addresses real user needs, I combined desk research, user interviews and on-site observations. This helped me better understand the cultural context of Aarhus' street art and how people navigate these locations.

The interviews were crucial since they revealed that what users value is:

  • Visual inspiration to decide whether they might be interested
  • Contextual information about the locations, such as the story between each artwork and its artist
  • Simple navigation, to avoid wasting time on their phones while exploring

Most participants relied on their smartphones when exploring the city and wanted a tool that could support route planning. Through observations of the spots, I realized that accessibility and visibility of each location should be highlighted as well.

01.2 Target Group

Based on the research findings, the website was designed for young people aged 15–30, primarily students, young professionals, and international visitors in Aarhus.

01.3 Problem Statement

How can I design and develop a responsive experience website that helps users discover three hidden street art locations in Aarhus, while providing relevant information, strong visual storytelling, and an intuitive user experience across both mobile and desktop devices?

02.1 User Persona

The first thing I did was create a user persona to translate my research findings into a realistic user who represents my target group, so that it would be easier to center my decisions on the user’s pains and needs.

02.2 Values

The project focused on:

  • Creativity to celebrate artistic expression and visual diversity
  • Community to connect people through a shared interest in street art
  • Adventure to encourage exploration and discovery
  • Safety to provide clear and reliable information
  • Youthfulness to have a fresh and modern approach
  • Local appreciation to highlight the hidden side of the city of Aarhus
  • Underground to appreciate less well-known places and expressions

02.3 Define the User’s Needs and Problems

With this information in mind, I formulated some How Might We questions to brainstorm how to translate user needs into functionalities.

HOW MIGHT WE...

  • Help users discover hidden or lesser-known street art without taking away the sense of exploration?
  • Make street art approachable for users who don’t know anything about graffiti culture?
  • Make it easy for users to share spots or photos with friends and on social media?
  • Present just the right amount of information without overwhelming users on mobile screens?
  • Make it easy and safe for tourists to navigate to graffiti spots in unfamiliar neighborhoods?

Lastly I created a Value Proposition Canvas to organize all of the insights I gathered. This helped ensure that the solution was solving real user needs and delivering a meaningful experience.

ThE USEr:

Jobs:

Discover hidden street art, learn the stories behind the artworks, plan visits, and navigate safely.

Gains:

Easy navigation, inspiring visuals, meaningful context, and a fun, memorable exploration experience.

Pains:

Hidden locations are difficult to find, information is often limited, and existing websites can feel cluttered or difficult to use on mobile.

ThE SOLUTION:

Products & Services:

A responsive website featuring an interactive map, curated location pages, photography, artist stories, and mobile-friendly navigation.

Pain Relievers:

Clear directions, concise content, intuitive UI, accessibility considerations, and contextual information for each location.

Gain Creators:

High-quality visuals, engaging storytelling, smooth interactions, and a curated experience that encourages exploration while preserving the feeling of discovery.

02.4 Design Process and User Testing

• Sketches and User Flow

Initial sketches were used to quickly explore different layouts and navigation ideas before creating wireframes, helping identify the most intuitive user experience.

The research highlighted that the target group is split into two when deciding what to visit: some users prefer to casually browse the list of artworks to see which ones they like, while others are on a strict schedule and want to see where the spots are on the map.

I created the user flow keeping that in mind, so I added two CTAs on the homepage that navigate to The Walls page, where users can see both the map and the gallery at the same time.

• Visual Identity

The moodboard defined the project's visual direction, capturing the creative and urban atmosphere of Aarhus' street art.

The style tile established a consistent visual identity through typography, colors, and UI elements used across the website.

• Wireframes

To test whether the user flow I had in mind could work, I created the wireframes following a mobile-first approach.

02.8 Usability Testing

The prototype was tested with five users to evaluate the overall user experience and gather feedback on usability, navigation, and content clarity before moving on to the final design.

Key Findings

  • Interactive map and visual storytelling were highly engaging.
  • Navigation between pages was intuitive and easy to follow.
  • Increase font sizes to improve readability.
  • Simplify the homepage layout to reduce visual clutter.
  • Add brief instructions for map filters and pins.
  • Keep artist stories and interactive features as core elements of the experience.

Take-aways and Further Development Ideas

I really enjoyed working on this project. Street art is a theme I am very passionate about, and it was interesting to research it more. It was also a good opportunity to connect with people who share the same interest, who greatly helped me with feedback and recommendations throughout the whole process.

At the time, I was just starting as a designer and I wasn’t very confident in Figma yet, so there are some things I would slightly change, like making the CTAs on the homepage more prominent. That being said, I’m still very proud of the project’s visual identity, and it taught me a lot about creating a cohesive design system and designing custom icons.
